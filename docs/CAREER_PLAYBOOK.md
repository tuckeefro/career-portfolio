# Career packet: how to use the work

The lead is mechanical engineering carried into equipment setup, operation, and troubleshooting. The selected evidence comes from production startup, waterjet repair, and prototype instrumentation. Earlier shop and practical operations work supports that lead; independent projects add separate examples.

## Interview story: production air supply

> The facility was still being built out while production equipment was arriving. I needed to get the first line supplied under a tight startup budget while allowing for another line. I used the machine airflow requirements, included the planned second line and headroom, and selected a rented KAESER AIRCENTER package. I integrated the filtration and distribution and carried that work into line build and operation. The supply kept up during break-in and the initial production run without another receiver purchase. I accepted higher compressor duty cycle. The second line had not been ordered, so its capacity was a design allowance.

Show page 2. Be ready to explain how machine demand was combined, why rental made sense at that stage, and what operating observation justified the receiver decision.

## Interview story: waterjet repair

> The waterjet was making a clean through-cut, then removing surface material for roughly an inch without cutting through. That was disrupting production. I investigated the high-pressure pump and found more mechanical play than expected in its cylinder assemblies. I performed the scheduled 5,000-hour rebuild using the standard kit, reassembled the pump, and checked the machine afterward. Consistent cutting returned, the pump output readings were higher, and production restarted after three working days. The useful part of the story is the full repair: inspection, rebuild, reassembly, and checking the result.

Show page 3. Be ready to identify what moved, what the kit replaced, and which checks supported restarting the machine. The exact failed part is not established in the retained account.

## Interview story: prototype instrumentation

> At Maybell I worked on the refrigeration prototype's instrumentation and operator interface. I set up and wired the Lake Shore 372, brought its readout through a LattePanda, and combined it with pressure, flow, heater output, and valve state in a single GUI. Operators could use the same interface to command pneumatic valves in the gas-handling system. My contribution connected the instrument, its readout, and the interface used to monitor and operate the prototype. I also worked on connector-assembly trials and CNC-welder positioning; that welding setup did not have the precision needed for that experimental phase.

Show page 4. Be ready to explain the actual instrument connection and valve-command path. The APS record identifies the team's cable presentation and Tucker's presenter/coauthor role; it is separate from proof of the instrumentation arrangement.

## Prepare one application at a time

1. Identify the opening's main equipment, production, test, or service duties.
2. Choose the matching case and relevant experience bullets.
3. Put the portfolio link near the relevant résumé experience; attach the PDF when the application accepts a work sample.
4. Rehearse the selected story once using the sheet. Explain your own contribution and the stage actually reached.
5. Confirm employer titles/dates and any current credential the application requires. Use the [firsthand-detail list](OWNER_INPUTS.md) to strengthen the technical follow-up.

The packet supports applications now. Original drawings, worksheets, or repair photos can strengthen the corresponding sheet as they become available.
